3 · Would one agent with all the tools be simpler?

Team of five vs. one agent

I don't think so. With this problem, agents can work at the same time and double-check each other, which leads to faster resolutions than one agent taking one step at a time. On the other hand, one agent would keep everything connected in one context, which may be stronger.

Evidence for the team
  • Working at the same time: on 103 the Boss had the price quote and the stock check running in parallel.
  • Double-checking:
    • On 102, Facilities verified the lease, and then Accounting separately confirmed that rent plus invoice 501 ($3,240) fit in $3,400.
    • On 101, Inventory caught that invoice 501 already covered the reprint before anyone ordered again.
  • Least privilege: Customer Service has no cash tools, so the replies on 101 and 103 couldn't mention cash, vendors or costs. A single agent would hold every tool, including prepare_payment.
What would have been simpler as one agent, and why (“interconnectivity”)
  • Ticket 102 as a whole. It is one straight line: read lease 1 → prepare the $2,400 rent → close. One agent holding get_lease, prepare_payment and set_ticket_status could do it in one loop, without a Facilities → Accounting hand-off. The rent wouldn't have been prepared twice.
  • The customer reply on 103. One agent would already know the discount was approved when it wrote to the club, so there'd be no rewrite.
  • The agents didn't coordinate on duplicates. The rent was prepared twice on 102, and invoice 501 was flagged by both 101 and 103. The backend's one-item-per-bill queue caught it, not the agents.
  • Context was lost in a hand-off. On 103, Customer Service drafted its reply before the Boss had approved the discount, so the Boss had to rewrite it.
  • Hand-offs cost time on the hard ticket. 101 and 102 each finished in 6 to 7 s. 103 took 102.7 s and about 94.6k tokens, with Accounting run twice.

The team is the better fit for this shop's separated duties: stock, money, lease and customers. The weak spot is shared context, and a shared “what's already prepared” list helps close it.

5 · Three new problems this team could not solve

Where the app runs out

  1. Troubleshooting with a customer to find the best solution when an item is out of stock: do they want to wait, or choose something else?
  2. Accounting for incoming cash from the in-person store together with the online store.
  3. Bringing in new items (for example, frisbees) and adding and accounting for them in all the right categories.
Why this app can't do it today
  • A back-and-forth conversation. Customer Service writes one reply and the Boss closes the ticket, and there's no tool to read the customer's answer.
    • On 101 it offered M (5), L (3) or XL (2) instead of waiting for the S.
    • On 103 it offered S/L/XL instead of waiting for the 12 M.
    • In both cases the customer's choice can't come back into the app.
  • Cash coming in. On the Cash tab, cash only ever goes down, on approvals. There is no sales or revenue table and no MCP tool that records income.
    • The $986 hoodie sale and the $28 tee never reach checking.
    • That's why the $264 restock is stuck at $160.
  • New products. Every MCP tool is read-only, except the Boss's set_ticket_status.
    • No agent can create a SKU, set a cost and list price, add sizes or stock, or pick a vendor for a new category.
    • The catalog is fixed: 4 priced SKUs and 10 stock rows.
    • A frisbee order gets “Unknown SKU” from check_stock_for_order (I tested it: the only known SKUs are the hat, hoodie, mug and tee). It would need a human, plus new tables and tools.
